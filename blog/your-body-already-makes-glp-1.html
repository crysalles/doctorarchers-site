Your Body Already Makes GLP-1: The Natural Side of the Ozempic Conversation

July 10, 2026

Written and reviewed by Rev. Dr. LaVeena B. Archers, PhD
Doctor of Natural Medicine · Board-certified in Holistic Functional Medicine and Functional Nutrition
Educational content only. Not medical advice, and never a substitute for your own care. Scope of practice.

You cannot open a phone right now without hearing about the new weight-loss injections. What gets lost in the noise is a simple fact: the hormone those drugs mimic, GLP-1, is one your own body already makes, every day, in response to what and how you eat.

What GLP-1 is

GLP-1 is a gut hormone. After a meal, your intestines release it, and it does several helpful things. It tells your brain you are full, it slows how fast your stomach empties, and it helps steady your blood sugar. The medications work by flooding the body with a long-lasting version of that same signal.

What the drugs do, fairly

Let me be even-handed here. For some people, especially those carrying significant health risk, these drugs are close to life-changing, and they deserve to be seen as a legitimate tool rather than a moral failing. They also are not magic, they come with real considerations, and they work far better alongside good food and strength training than on their own. Any decision about them belongs with your own prescriber.

Supporting your own GLP-1, naturally

You can nudge your own GLP-1 with ordinary habits. This will never match a pharmaceutical dose, and it is still real, and it is free:

  • Protein and fiber at meals, which prompt a stronger GLP-1 response
  • Whole, minimally processed food your gut recognizes
  • A well-tended gut, since the bacteria living there influence the signal
  • Movement, which improves how your whole metabolic system responds

This is the same foundation that supports everything else in your health, pointed at one useful hormone.

A grounded middle

The most useful place to stand is in the middle: fair to the medicine, and clear that the natural path supports your own GLP-1 without pretending to replace the drugs. That is exactly the ground my next book, Your Body's Own GLP-1, is being written to cover, in full and without hype.
