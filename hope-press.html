The Publisher

Hope Press

The independent imprint behind the root-cause guides. Small, deliberately so, and built around one rule: if a book says the research shows something, you should be able to go and check.

What Hope Press is, plainly

Hope Press is my own imprint. It publishes my books and, for now, only my books. I would rather say that on the front page than let anyone assume there is a building full of editors behind it.

That matters because "published by" is a phrase people read as a credential, and in this corner of publishing it very often is not one. A great many health books carry an imprint name that exists solely to avoid the words "self-published." I am not doing that. The name on the spine is mine, the decisions are mine, and the mistakes are mine.

So the honest question is not who publishes these books. It is what the publisher actually guarantees. Below is the list, and it is the same list whether you buy a book here, on Amazon, or borrow it from a library.

What every Hope Press book carries

These are not aspirations. Each one is either in the book or the book does not go out.

Every book has

  • A tag on nearly every factual claim, marking whether it was shown in people, observed only as an association, demonstrated in animals alone, tested and found not to hold, or simply not studied.
  • A dagger where the evidence came from someone else — from men, or from women much older or younger than the reader. An enormous amount of women's health advice rests on research done in young men, and where that is true the book says so on the sentence itself.
  • A counted tally of its own claims, made from the finished manuscript rather than estimated.
  • Sources named at the end of each chapter, by DOI or PubMed record, so a claim can be checked without taking my word for it.
  • A dated medical disclaimer and a plain statement of what my credentials are and are not.

No Hope Press book will

  • Promise to cure, reverse, or treat a disease.
  • Tell you to start, change, or stop a medication.
  • Use a composite character or an invented patient story to make a point land harder.
  • Cite a podcast, a blog post, or another book where a primary source was what the claim needed.
  • Flatten unsettled research into "studies show." Where a field is genuinely undecided, the book says it is undecided.

The full method — where the evidence comes from, how each piece is reviewed, how AI is used in the research, and how errors get corrected — is set out separately.

Rights, permissions, and review copies

Hope Press handles its own rights. If you are a journalist, a librarian, a book club, a clinician who wants to quote a passage, or a publisher interested in translation or audio rights, the contact form reaches me directly and I answer it myself.

  • Review copies Available to reviewers, librarians, and health journalists. Say which title and which format suits you.
  • Permissions Brief quotation in a review needs no permission. Anything longer, or any reproduction in teaching material, just ask — the answer is usually yes.
  • Translation and audio rights Unsold for every title. Enquiries welcome.
  • Corrections If a source does not say what a book claims it says, that is a defect and I want to know. Corrections are logged publicly, not quietly patched.
