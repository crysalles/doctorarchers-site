Every claim is labelled for how well it is actually supported

Most health books state everything in the same tone of voice. A finding from a large human trial sounds exactly like a mechanism observed in mice, which sounds exactly like the author's opinion. You have no way to tell them apart, and that is usually the point.

Each substantive sentence in this book is followed by a tag. It describes the evidence behind that sentence, not behind the book as a whole.

  • [SHOWN IN PEOPLE] Tested directly in human beings, usually in a controlled trial, and it held up.
  • [ASSOCIATED ONLY] People with this tend to have that outcome, but nobody has run the experiment that would show cause.
  • [NOT STUDIED] Either nobody has properly tested it, or the claim is my own reasoning rather than a finding of its own.
  • [TESTED, DIDN'T HOLD] Researchers ran a proper study and it did not work. That is a finding, not a gap.
  • [ANIMALS ONLY] The evidence comes from rodents or cells, not from human trials.
  • † Shown, but in people older than you, or in men, or in some group you do not belong to.

Tested, didn't hold and not studied are the pair most often confused, so the book states it plainly: tested-and-failed means somebody checked and it failed. Not studied means nobody checked. A product that has been tested and failed is very often sold as though nobody had gotten around to looking yet.

The book makes 1,061 marked claims. Seven hundred and two of them are not marked shown in people. Twenty-one more were shown in people, but in men, or in people older than you.

Fewer than a third of the claims in this book were demonstrated in people like you.

That is not a failure of the research behind it. It is the honest state of this field, and it is the reason the book needed writing. Open it at any page and check.
