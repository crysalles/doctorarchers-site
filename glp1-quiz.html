Is a GLP-1 worth discussing for you?

Ten questions to organise your own thinking before you sit down with a prescriber. This is not a medical test and it cannot tell you what to do. It can stop you walking into that appointment with nothing but a feeling.

Before you score anything

Some histories change this conversation completely, and no quiz can weigh them properly. If any of the following apply to you, they belong at the front of your discussion with a prescriber rather than buried in a total at the bottom of a page:

  • A personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, or of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2
  • A history of pancreatitis
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Significant gastrointestinal disease, including gastroparesis
  • A history of disordered eating, at any point in your life
  • Pregnancy, planning a pregnancy, or breastfeeding

None of these is a reason you cannot be helped. They are reasons the conversation starts somewhere other than where this page starts. Please raise them first.

How this works

Answer all ten questions and you will get two scores rather than one, because they answer two different questions.

The first is about whether this question is live for you, given your health, what you have already tried and what it did. The second is about whether it would hold up in your actual life, given cost, muscle protection and whether you have a prescriber to partner with.

Keeping them apart matters. Whether a medication suits your body and whether you could sustain it are separate questions, and adding them into one number hides both.

The bands have no clinical validation behind their cutoffs. They are a way to organise your thinking, nothing more.

Part one: is the question live for you?

1. Where is your weight relative to a healthy range for your body?
2. Which of these has a doctor actually diagnosed in you? Choose the single tier that fits best.
3. How long, and how seriously, have you worked the foundations? By foundations I mean real food, protein, strength training, sleep and stress care.
4. What have the foundations done for you so far?
5. Which is closest to your real reason for considering any of this?
6. How is your daily life going right now, meaning your energy, mobility, breath and sleep?

Part two: would it hold up in your life?

7. Could you sustain the cost of a GLP-1 medicine, financially and logistically, for the years it may take?
8. How ready are you to protect your muscle and bone through this, whichever path you take? That means protein at each meal and regular strength work.
9. How do you feel about a GLP-1 medicine as a tool, setting your own numbers aside?
10. What is your access to a prescriber who could partner with you on this?

Whatever you decide, one thing holds

Roughly a quarter of the weight lost on these medications is lean mass, and for a woman in midlife that matters more than the number on the scale. Protein and resistance training are what decide whether you come out the other side lighter and strong, or simply smaller. That is true whether you take a medication or never do.

What this page is, and what it is not

I am a Doctor of Natural Medicine. I do not prescribe, and I cannot tell you whether a GLP-1 medication belongs in your body. That decision sits with you and a qualified prescriber who knows your history, your family history, your cardiovascular and clotting risk, and everything this page cannot see.

This is a reflection exercise adapted from my forthcoming book Your Body's Own GLP-1. It is education, not medical advice, not diagnosis, and not a recommendation in either direction. Nothing here is a reason to start a medication, and nothing here is a reason to stop one you have been prescribed.
